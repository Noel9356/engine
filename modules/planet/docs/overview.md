# planet module

planet generates procedural 3D planets: a signed density field shaped by continents, eroded mountains (an analytic erosion filter whose directional gully stripes carve branching valleys and leave sharp ridges, blended at a configurable weight against the classic ridged profile), hills, terraces and true 3D displacement (overhangs, rock strata, optional caves), classified into geologic provinces, climates and biomes, meshed with marching cubes into LOD chunk meshes, and streamed through an octree that keeps the working set hugging the terrain shell. Every chunk mesh is a pure function of the planet parameters and the chunk's integer coordinates, which makes generation deterministic and independent of chunk build order. The terrain's cliff mask is derived from its own steepness (hill-flank bands and mountain crest lines), so terraced mesa country forms where the ground is genuinely steep.

## Capability

The module provides the `planet` capability (ABI version constant `planetCapabilityAbi = 2`, see `include/engine/planet/capabilities.hpp`; ABI 2 added a content hash to each chunk view so consumers detect rebuilt boundary meshes). Planet handles are `std::uint32_t`, never reused, with `0` (`invalidPlanet`) meaning "none"; the number of simultaneously live planets is bounded by the `max_live_planets` configuration key (`createPlanet` reports `CapacityReached` beyond it). `PlanetDescription` layers per-planet overrides (seed, radius, sea level offset, center) over the module's `[planet]` configuration; a validated `PlanetGenerationParams` snapshot is available through `copyGenerationParams`.

Entries: `createPlanet`, `destroyPlanet`, `updatePlanet` (per-frame LOD advance from an eye position), `visibleChunkCapacity` + `copyVisibleChunks` (borrowed views of the ready chunk meshes, valid until the next `updatePlanet`; each view carries the mesh's FNV-1a content hash), `sampleSurfaceRadius` (first density crossing along a unit direction), `sampleDensity` (signed field density at a world point), `sampleClimate` (biome/temperature/moisture at a surface point), `sampleRelief` (radial height plus the mountain/cliff masks - consumers can locate specific landforms such as terraced cliff country), `copyStatistics`, `copyGenerationParams`, and `hashChunk` (synchronous pure build + FNV-1a hash, the determinism self-test hook).

## Provided / required / optional capabilities

- `module.toml`: `provides = ["planet"]`, `requires = []`.
- Entry table (bottom of `source/planet_module.cpp`): one provided capability, no required or optional capabilities, no declared permissions.

## How it works

- **Determinism.** A chunk mesh is `f(params, coord, conformMask)`: sampling positions, gradient cells, climate colors and MC case tables are all pure functions of the planet parameters, the chunk's integer coordinates and the conforming mask (itself a pure function of the sorted visible list and the coordinate). No shared mutable state participates in a build, so builds can run on any thread in any order; the self-test (`demo_planet --self-test`) builds the same chunk set from three planets in ascending, reversed and rotated orders and compares hash multisets, plus seed-sensitivity and mask-variant (unconstrained / all-conforming / mixed) stability checks. Cross-run determinism follows from integer-exact lattice addressing (no accumulated floats).
- **Eroded mountains.** The mountain belts carry relief from an analytic erosion filter (see `erosion.hpp/.cpp`): every point is evaluated in isolation - there is no water state to simulate or carry between chunks - so the streaming octree can sample the same eroded field at any chunk size, in any build order, on any thread. The filter carves branching gullies into the massif flanks (directional stripes aligned with the slope), leaves sharp ridges between them, and preserves crisp peaks and valley floors through the stacked fading; the gully octaves band-limit per octave like every other noise stream, so coarse LOD shells read smooth far-field relief while close-range chunks keep the full gully stack. `mountain_erosion_weight` blends the eroded profile against the classic mass-and-crest profile at any point between them.
- **Overhangs.** The radial relief alone would be a wrapped heightmap. The overhang term adds `simplex3(point / overhang_wavelength) * overhang_amplitude * mask` to the signed density, displacing the iso-surface sideways as well as vertically; strata and (optional) caves are further 3D terms. Together they produce ledges, cave mouths and overhanging cliff faces that a heightmap cannot represent.
- **Terraced cliff country.** The cliff mask reads the terrain's own steepness (the hill field's flanks and the mountain crest lines, concentrated by `cliff_sharpness` and gated by `cliff_steepness_low/high`), so terracing, strata banding and the overhang/detail weighting land on flanks and crests instead of scattering over open ground. Inside the mask the height profile quantizes into benches joined by narrow RISERS (`terrace_riser_fraction` of each terrace step): the riser's grade is the flank slope divided by that fraction, so a 0.35-grade flank becomes a ~60-degree rock wall carrying strata stripes and overhang ledges while the treads stay flat benches. `sampleRelief` reports the mask, so consumers can locate terraced country the way the demo's cliffs view does.
- **Geologic provinces.** A very-low-frequency field classifies every landmass region into layered sedimentary country, shield/volcanic country and pale carbonate patches; the terrain's strata banding and terracing bias with the rock character and the bare-rock albedo tints to match, so cliff faces look and structure themselves as one geology. The province field rides the same band-limited machinery as every other term, so coarse LOD chunks neither spend the evaluation nor alias it.
- **Climate and biomes.** Temperature = equator-to-pole gradient (configurable `equator_temperature` / `pole_temperature`) minus an altitude lapse rate, perturbed by a noise field; moisture blends a base humidity field (domain-warped so biome patches read as organic, elongated shapes) with the circulation-cell latitude belts (ITCZ wet belt, subtropical dry belts, temperate westerlies, polar highs), continental-interior drying keyed to the continent field itself (`climate_continentality_strength`) and a rain shadow that samples the mountain mass envelope upwind along the prevailing wind (`climate_rain_shadow_strength`, with `climate_wind_zones_enabled` following the easterly-trade / westerly / polar-easterly latitude zones). Biomes classify Whittaker-style from the pair, with continuous blending between palette rows instead of hard per-vertex switches.
- **Surface walking.** `SurfaceWalker` moves a point-feet character across the terrain with planet-centric gravity: up is the radial direction at the character, movement lives in the tangent plane, and the ground under the feet is found by marching the same pure density field the mesher samples (inward from the feet, outward when the feet end up buried), then bisecting the bracket - so overhang undersides and cave floors carry the feet exactly like the rendered surface does, with no collision proxy and nothing to keep in sync. Movement attempts are limited by a step-up window and a climb grade, steep walls block and slide along their axis, drops beyond the snap window become falls, jumps integrate against gravity, and deep water floats the character at the sea surface (shallow water stays walkable) - and only when the water column between the feet and the waterline reads open, so cave pockets carved under the sea floor keep the ground logic instead of floating the feet up through the rock. No physics module is needed: the field answers every ground query directly, the controller is kinematic and pure, and fixed inputs reproduce one exact path (asserted by the demo's `--self-test`).
- **Streaming and LOD.** Each frame `updatePlanet` re-selects the visible node set for the eye - skipping the descent entirely while the eye has moved less than `selection_update_distance_meters` (the derived finest voxel by default), which pins the whole selection under a walking camera - requests missing meshes in apparent-size order (widest on screen first), cancels queued builds the new selection no longer renders, and drains finished builds. Selection output is sorted by (depth, x, y, z) so the visible list itself is deterministic; per-chunk MESHES are pure functions anyway, which is what makes generation order-independent.
- **Streaming coverage.** A ground view selects thousands of chunks and the build queue takes seconds to minutes to serve them all, so a fresh view must not show empty sky in the meantime. When `lod_missing_fallback` is on, a visible chunk without a finished mesh draws its nearest finished ancestor whose LOD suits its distance (the selection's own screen-space rule with a 2x tolerance, measured to the ancestor's piece of the terrain shell, so a coarse mesh can never stand in beside or underneath the camera), and the streamer additionally requests the chunk's coarsest PURE ancestor - the coarsest ancestor under which no finished visible chunk lies, so its mesh covers the hole without overlapping finished geometry - under a per-update budget (`lod_scaffold_builds_per_update`). Every request, fine or scaffold, competes on the same apparent-screen-size order, so coverage arrives coarse at range and fine near the eye (chunks closer to the viewer stream smaller, exactly like the selection itself), the visible planet sharpens in place as the fine meshes land, and the finished steady state pays nothing: the stamping and planning passes run only on updates where at least one visible chunk is still missing.
- **Seam-free chunk boundaries.** Three mechanisms replace the earlier skirt walls (which read as painted dark stripes between chunks):
  - *Shared lattice.* Same-depth neighbors evaluate bit-identical positions and gradients for every boundary vertex, so their meshes weld exactly - nothing to hide.
  - *2:1 octree balance + fresh-record balancing.* Without balance, the distance rule can place rendered leaves 2+ depth levels apart across a shared face; the coarse chord then departs from the fine isoline over several voxels and LOD rings show cracks. After each selection, a fixpoint walks the 26 face/edge/corner neighbor directions of every rendered leaf, finds the shallowest covering leaf on each neighbor chain, and force-splits the coarse chain until adjacent leaves differ by at most one level. The walk only trusts records refreshed by the CURRENT selection: records left over from earlier eye positions describe a tree that no longer exists, and trusting them silently skips violations (order-dependent crack dashes at the rings). Balancing changes WHICH chunks render, never their geometry.
  - *Conforming boundary sampling + extension strips (cross-depth transitions).* Wherever a face segment borders a rendered leaf one level coarser (the per-segment `ConformMask`), the fine chunk samples that segment's boundary-plane lattice points from the COARSE LATTICE's trilinear interpolant instead of the raw field - at coarse lattice points this degenerates to the coarse sample itself, so the fine isoline and the coarse chords share their exact zero crossings on every coarse grid line. The residual between crossings (the coarse chord sagitta) is covered by the EXTENSION: the fine chunk also marches the apron cells just beyond conforming faces, carrying the true surface INTO the coarse neighbor's territory, where the neighbor's own surface overlaps it. The two surfaces interpenetrate along the whole transition, so no viewing ray can slip between them - the sub-voxel mismatch reads as terrain-colored overlap, never as a crack against the background. The conform decision is SYMMETRIC: it ORs the two depth-(d-1) cells adjacent to the plane at the point's diagonal position (one seen from each side), because the two sides would otherwise consult different cells and disagree at chunk corners. Meshes remain pure functions of `(params, coord, conformMask)`.

## Threading and lifetime

`updatePlanet`, `copyVisibleChunks` and the sampling calls are main-thread (capability calls are main-thread only). Chunk builds run on the module's own worker pool (one thread per permitted concurrent build) against the pure build path; results flow through a mutex-guarded handoff list into the main-thread cache. `Planet` owns a copy of its params (the capability receives descriptions as stack temporaries), and `~Planet` stops the pool and drains in-flight builds before the field dies. Borrowed chunk views stay valid until the next `updatePlanet` call. `SurfaceWalker` is main-thread state over a shared pure field: any number of walkers can read one `DensityField` from any thread, but each walker's own update/teleport calls belong to one thread.
