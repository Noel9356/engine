# planet module

planet generates procedural 3D planets: a signed density field shaped by continents, ridged mountains, hills, terraces and true 3D displacement (overhangs, rock strata, optional caves), classified into climates and biomes, meshed with marching cubes into LOD chunk meshes, and streamed through an octree that keeps the working set hugging the terrain shell. Every chunk mesh is a pure function of the planet parameters and the chunk's integer coordinates, which makes generation deterministic and independent of chunk build order. The terrain's cliff mask is derived from its own steepness (hill-flank bands and mountain crest lines), so terraced mesa country forms where the ground is genuinely steep.

## Capability

The module provides the `planet` capability (ABI version constant `planetCapabilityAbi = 2`, see `include/engine/planet/capabilities.hpp`; ABI 2 added a content hash to each chunk view so consumers detect rebuilt boundary meshes). Planet handles are `std::uint32_t`, never reused, with `0` (`invalidPlanet`) meaning "none"; the number of simultaneously live planets is bounded by the `max_live_planets` configuration key (`createPlanet` reports `CapacityReached` beyond it). `PlanetDescription` layers per-planet overrides (seed, radius, sea level offset, center) over the module's `[planet]` configuration; a validated `PlanetGenerationParams` snapshot is available through `copyGenerationParams`.

Entries: `createPlanet`, `destroyPlanet`, `updatePlanet` (per-frame LOD advance from an eye position), `visibleChunkCapacity` + `copyVisibleChunks` (borrowed views of the ready chunk meshes, valid until the next `updatePlanet`; each view carries the mesh's FNV-1a content hash), `sampleSurfaceRadius` (first density crossing along a unit direction), `sampleDensity` (signed field density at a world point), `sampleClimate` (biome/temperature/moisture at a surface point), `sampleRelief` (radial height plus the mountain/cliff masks - consumers can locate specific landforms such as terraced cliff country), `copyStatistics`, `copyGenerationParams`, and `hashChunk` (synchronous pure build + FNV-1a hash, the determinism self-test hook).

## Provided / required / optional capabilities

- `module.toml`: `provides = ["planet"]`, `requires = []`.
- Entry table (bottom of `source/planet_module.cpp`): one provided capability, no required or optional capabilities, no declared permissions.

| `include/engine/planet/climate.hpp/.cpp` | `ClimateSampler`: latitude/altitude temperature with a perturbation field, moisture from noise plus latitude belts (ITCZ, subtropical highs), Whittaker-style biome classification, and CONTINUOUS biome color blending - albedo interpolates between palette rows over configurable blend widths (temperature/moisture, slope, elevation bands) instead of hard-switching, because per-vertex hard switches smear into pale stains across triangles. Sharp SPATIAL BANDS (the beach strip, the wet-sand rim, the altitude snow-line crossing) fade out once their width drops below the color's sample spacing, the same band-limiting the albedo jitter applies: at coarse LOD the bands would otherwise alias into isolated off-color speckle (dark coastal fringes, cloud-like snow patches), while ground-level views keep them at full strength. |
| `include/engine/planet/density.hpp/.cpp` | `DensityField`: the signed density (meters). Radial relief (domain-warped continents, ridged mountains masked by the continent field, hills, terraces with a cliff mask) plus true 3D terms: overhang displacement, strata banding, optional cave worms. Every layer is evaluated only where its mask survives: mountains are skipped over the oceans (the mask test precedes the two most expensive layers), and the detail fields, overhangs and strata are skipped under water and where their masks fade to zero. The CLIFF MASK is derived from the terrain's own steepness - the hill field's flanks (its zero-crossing bands) and the mountain crest lines, sharpened by `cliff_sharpness` and gated by `cliff_steepness_low/high` - so terracing, strata banding and overhang weighting land on genuinely steep ground and `sampleRelief`'s cliff factor is a meaningful steepness signal for consumers. Terraces quantize the height profile inside the mask with a narrow riser (`terrace_riser_fraction` of each step), producing flat benches joined by cliff-like walls. A submarine fade keeps ocean basins smooth; a coastal-flatten band damps the detail fields toward the waterline so shorelines slope into the sea instead of breaking into sub-sea pond webs. The ocean floor sinks from the shoreline toward `abyssal_depth_meters` as the continent field dips into its basins (an ocean mask riding the band-limited continent field), carving real ocean profiles - a sunlit continental shelf at the coast, a slope, dark abyssal plains offshore - so the water's depth tint reads as turquoise shelves fringing navy open water. `surfaceRadius` returns the FIRST density crossing along a direction (march to the first solid-to-air bracket, then bisect inside it - the crossing a camera or probe hits, even when overhangs or caves add further crossings above it); `reliefAt` exposes height plus the mountain/cliff masks (the capability's `sampleRelief`). |
| `include/engine/planet/meshing.hpp/.cpp` + `source/marching_cubes_tables.hpp` | Marching cubes per chunk over a `(n+3)^3` padded grid (the 256-entry Bourke edge/triangle tables live in the internal `marching_cubes_tables.hpp`, shared by the terrain and water passes). All sample positions derive from ONE global lattice index per axis (`-half + step * globalIndex`, steps related by exact powers of two), so any two chunks compute bit-identical positions for shared lattice points: same-depth neighbors weld watertight, cross-depth boundaries stay aligned. Vertex normals average the gradients of the (up to) four cells adjacent to the edge in canonical min-corner form - still a pure function of the edge's global position, but continuous across cell borders (a single-cell gradient is constant per cell and speckles steep creases with dark facets). Biome colors come from the climate sampler's blended palette, with the albedo jitter band-limited at the chunk's VERTEX spacing (sub-spacing mottle fades out on coarse LOD chunks instead of aliasing into speckle). Cross-depth boundaries are sealed by CONFORMING BOUNDARY SAMPLING plus the EXTENSION STRIP (see "Seam-free chunk boundaries" below): on face segments whose far cell is a rendered leaf one level coarser, the boundary-plane lattice points sample the coarse lattice's trilinear interpolant instead of the raw field, and the chunk also marches the apron cells just beyond those faces so its surface carries the true isoline into the coarse neighbor's territory, where the two surfaces interpenetrate. Edge crossings clamp the interpolation denominator (identical endpoint densities must not produce inf/NaN vertices - a degenerate triangle spans the whole screen in a software rasterizer). The water pass marches the sea-sphere field with depth-tinted colors and radially auto-oriented triangles; its depth tint samples `densityAt` directly with PER-CHANNEL saturation depths (water absorbs red fastest, green next, blue last, so the tint runs shallow turquoise -> green-teal mid shelf -> navy deep), a noise-modulated surf band keys to the `water_foam_*` keys, a deterministic two-scale wave field (swell plus half-wavelength chop) tilts the normals at close range, fully buried water cells are skipped so the sea sphere cannot poke grazing-angle slivers through the terrain, and the pass runs only for nodes whose bounding volume reaches the sea shell. Open-ocean vertices whose whole lattice edge already sits beyond the tint's saturation depth skip the exact field evaluation (the tint reads the deep color from there down, and the surf and shore blends only live within a few meters of the shoreline), so sea-heavy chunks mesh at a fraction of the coastal cost. The water surface freezes where the sea-level climate temperature drops below `sea_ice_temperature` (the field that also drives the land's freeze line, so ice caps meet snowy shores along one organic edge), and very shallow flats blend toward the beach-sand tone transmitted by the water's own hue (`water_shore_mix_meters`), so shelves read as sandy turquoise instead of one pale sheet. Terrain and water share ONE marching-cubes cell walk (corner classification, edge canonicalization, zero-crossing interpolation, edge-vertex dedup and the case-table traversal); the passes differ only in their field, cell domain, vertex assembly and triangle orientation, supplied as callables. Terrain UVs use a spherical mapping around the configured spin axis (longitude/latitude follow the same axis as climate latitude), so a tilted axis tilts the mapping with it. |
| `include/engine/planet/octree.hpp/.cpp` | LOD selection: shell-band pruning, solid/air probe pruning of subtrees (each node's 27 lattice points - corners, edge midpoints, face centers, center - are sampled on the node's band-limited field; when every probe sits beyond a per-depth sound margin on the same side of the isolevel, the node's interior cannot contain surface, and nodes straddling the sea surface are exempt so the ocean cannot get holes. The margin is precomputed per depth from the band-limited gradient bound and the bounded 3D terms, so it covers exactly the field variation a node of that depth can hold), horizon culling (a node is culled when all 8 cube corners sit in the shadow cone of the guaranteed-solid inner shell sphere - the occluded set is a convex cone, so the corner test is exact; the test is bypassed when the eye itself sits inside the occluder ball), and screen-space distance refinement (a node refines while its voxel subtends more than `lod_angular_voxel_radians` at the eye, so detail follows apparent size at any planet radius). A 2:1 balancing fixpoint forces splits along coarse neighbor chains until adjacent rendered leaves differ by at most one depth level; the balancing walks only records refreshed by the CURRENT selection (stale records from earlier eye positions are skipped and pruned, so live balancing cannot silently miss violations). `computeConformMask` derives, per rendered leaf, which face segments border a coarser rendered leaf - the segments are decided from GLOBAL indices (perpendicular + tangential coarse-cell coordinates, diagonal ring included) and the decision ORs the cells on BOTH sides of the plane, so every chunk touching the same boundary point computes the same mask bits. Visible leaves are returned sorted by (depth, x, y, z). |
| `include/engine/planet/atmosphere.hpp` / `source/atmosphere.cpp` | Pure sky-backdrop model: a zenith-to-horizon gradient keyed to the view direction's elevation above the local horizon, an altitude weight that fades the whole sky to space over `atmosphere_height_meters`, and a sun-elevation tint (day neutral, low-sun gold, night blue). The same header exposes `CloudField`: a band-limited coverage field sampled on a spherical shell at `cloud_altitude_meters`, thresholded so `cloud_coverage` approximates the sky fraction under cloud, with a fine breakup octave for ragged cumulus edges, a seed-derived wind frame that stretches the banks into elongated streaks with a low-frequency swirl bending their shapes, a very-low-frequency weather field that organizes the banks into large cloudy and clear systems, and a latitude-band term keyed to the spin axis (a wet equatorial belt, dry subtropics, stormy mid-latitudes). `cloud_edge_softness` sets the threshold band's width (crisp clumps vs soft decks). Stateless and thread-safe; the demo paints a gradient dome around the eye with it, feeds the horizon color to the renderer as the frame clear color, and meshes the cloud shell for its globe and orbit views (the shell is built for cameras outside it; a camera between the surface and the shell would see the far side of the sphere through the sky above the horizon). |

## How it works

- **Determinism.** A chunk mesh is `f(params, coord, conformMask)`: sampling positions, gradient cells, climate colors and MC case tables are all pure functions of the planet parameters, the chunk's integer coordinates and the conforming mask (itself a pure function of the sorted visible list and the coordinate). No shared mutable state participates in a build, so builds can run on any thread in any order; the self-test (`demo_planet --self-test`) builds the same chunk set from three planets in ascending, reversed and rotated orders and compares hash multisets, plus seed-sensitivity and mask-variant (unconstrained / all-conforming / mixed) stability checks. Cross-run determinism follows from integer-exact lattice addressing (no accumulated floats).
- **Overhangs.** The radial relief alone would be a wrapped heightmap. The overhang term adds `simplex3(point / overhang_wavelength) * overhang_amplitude * mask` to the signed density, displacing the iso-surface sideways as well as vertically; strata and (optional) caves are further 3D terms. Together they produce ledges, cave mouths and overhanging cliff faces that a heightmap cannot represent.
- **Terraced cliff country.** The cliff mask reads the terrain's own steepness (the hill field's flanks and the mountain crest lines, concentrated by `cliff_sharpness` and gated by `cliff_steepness_low/high`), so terracing, strata banding and the overhang/detail weighting land on flanks and crests instead of scattering over open ground. Inside the mask the height profile quantizes into benches joined by narrow RISERS (`terrace_riser_fraction` of each terrace step): the riser's grade is the flank slope divided by that fraction, so a 0.35-grade flank becomes a ~60-degree rock wall carrying strata stripes and overhang ledges while the treads stay flat benches. `sampleRelief` reports the mask, so consumers can locate terraced country the way the demo's cliffs view does.
- **Climate and biomes.** Temperature = equator-to-pole gradient (configurable `equator_temperature` / `pole_temperature`) minus an altitude lapse rate, perturbed by a noise field. Moisture = base humidity noise plus latitude belts. The (temperature, moisture) pair classifies through a Whittaker-style table into the `Biome` enum; water depth bands, beaches (with a wet-sand swash band above the waterline), bare-rock slopes (slope > `rock_slope_threshold`) and an altitude snow line override the table. The rendered color, however, is a CONTINUOUS blend of the palette rows: classification thresholds pick the biome id for consumers, while the albedo interpolates across the same thresholds over the configured blend widths - smooth coasts, snow lines and rock edges without per-vertex stains.
- **Surface walking.** `SurfaceWalker` moves a point-feet character across the terrain with planet-centric gravity: up is the radial direction at the character, movement lives in the tangent plane, and the ground under the feet is found by marching the same pure density field the mesher samples (inward from the feet, outward when the feet end up buried), then bisecting the bracket - so overhang undersides and cave floors carry the feet exactly like the rendered surface does, with no collision proxy and nothing to keep in sync. Movement attempts are limited by a step-up window and a climb grade, steep walls block and slide along their axis, drops beyond the snap window become falls, jumps integrate against gravity, and deep water floats the character at the sea surface (shallow water stays walkable) - and only when the water column between the feet and the waterline reads open, so cave pockets carved under the sea floor keep the ground logic instead of floating the feet up through the rock. No physics module is needed: the field answers every ground query directly, the controller is kinematic and pure, and fixed inputs reproduce one exact path (asserted by the demo's `--self-test`).
- **Streaming and LOD.** Each frame `updatePlanet` re-selects the visible node set for the eye, requests missing meshes in apparent-size order (widest on screen first) and drains finished builds. Selection is a pure function of the eye: when the eye is unchanged (a static camera, or frames that only drain builds), the descent, the balancing fixpoint and the conform-mask pass are skipped entirely; when additionally nothing was delivered and eviction is not due, the whole visible-set pass is skipped and the update costs a constant handful of checks. Refinement stops once a node's voxel subtends at most `lod_angular_voxel_radians` at the eye, so detail concentrates where it is visible; shell-band and probe pruning drop the solid interior and the empty sky (each pruned node's 27 lattice points are sampled with a per-depth margin that soundly bounds the band-limited field's variation inside the node); horizon culling removes the planet's far side (exact shadow-cone test against the guaranteed-solid inner sphere, safe for eyes at or near the surface). Builds run on the module's dedicated worker pool; `updatePlanet` itself never builds a chunk unless the workers have delivered nothing for a sustained run of updates while work is queued, in which case it builds one chunk on the calling thread so the visible set still converges on machines that give the workers no CPU.
- **Seam-free chunk boundaries.** Three mechanisms replace the earlier skirt walls (which read as painted dark stripes between chunks):
  - *Shared lattice.* Same-depth neighbors evaluate bit-identical positions and gradients for every boundary vertex, so their meshes weld exactly - nothing to hide.
  - *2:1 octree balance + fresh-record balancing.* Without balance, the distance rule can place rendered leaves 2+ depth levels apart across a shared face; the coarse chord then departs from the fine isoline over several voxels and LOD rings show cracks. After each selection, a fixpoint walks the 26 face/edge/corner neighbor directions of every rendered leaf, finds the shallowest covering leaf on each neighbor chain, and force-splits the coarse chain until adjacent leaves differ by at most one level. The walk only trusts records refreshed by the CURRENT selection: records left over from earlier eye positions describe a tree that no longer exists, and trusting them silently skips violations (order-dependent crack dashes at the rings). Balancing changes WHICH chunks render, never their geometry.
  - *Conforming boundary sampling + extension strips (cross-depth transitions).* Wherever a face segment borders a rendered leaf one level coarser (the per-segment `ConformMask`), the fine chunk samples that segment's boundary-plane lattice points from the COARSE LATTICE's trilinear interpolant instead of the raw field - at coarse lattice points this degenerates to the coarse sample itself, so the fine isoline and the coarse chords share their exact zero crossings on every coarse grid line. The residual between crossings (the coarse chord sagitta) is covered by the EXTENSION: the fine chunk also marches the apron cells just beyond conforming faces, carrying the true surface INTO the coarse neighbor's territory, where the neighbor's own surface overlaps it. The two surfaces interpenetrate along the whole transition, so no viewing ray can slip between them - the sub-voxel mismatch reads as terrain-colored overlap, never as a crack against the background. The conform decision is SYMMETRIC: it ORs the two depth-(d-1) cells adjacent to the plane at the point's diagonal position (one seen from each side), because the two sides would otherwise consult different cells and disagree at chunk corners. Meshes remain pure functions of `(params, coord, conformMask)`.

## Threading and lifetime

`updatePlanet`, `copyVisibleChunks` and the sampling calls are main-thread (capability calls are main-thread only). Chunk builds run on the module's own worker pool (one thread per permitted concurrent build) against the pure build path; results flow through a mutex-guarded handoff list into the main-thread cache. `Planet` owns a copy of its params (the capability receives descriptions as stack temporaries), and `~Planet` stops the pool and drains in-flight builds before the field dies. Borrowed chunk views stay valid until the next `updatePlanet` call. `SurfaceWalker` is main-thread state over a shared pure field: any number of walkers can read one `DensityField` from any thread, but each walker's own update/teleport calls belong to one thread.

## Configuration

See `configuration.md` for every `[planet]` key with ranges and effects. All lengths are meters; planets scale to arbitrary radii by raising `radius_meters` together with the wavelengths/amplitudes that should scale with it.
