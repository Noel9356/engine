# lumen_reflection module

lumen_reflection implements the engine's dynamic reflections following the architecture of Unreal Engine 5's Lumen Reflections: a stochastic, GGX-importance-sampled reflection solve at reduced screen resolution, resolved through a hybrid tracing pipeline (screen traces first, then scene ray tracing, skylight on miss), with hit lighting that carries the scene's diffuse global illumination, and a three-stage screen-space denoising chain (BRDF-weighted spatial reuse, reprojection-based temporal accumulation, variance-driven bilateral filtering). The implementation is CPU-side (task-scheduler parallel); the renderer-facing result is the screen-space reflection field exposed through `copyField`, which the renderer composites onto shaded pixels as the indirect specular term.

## What Lumen Reflections actually do (and what this module adapts)

Lumen's reflections (per Epic's published design - the SIGGRAPH 2022 Advances course "Lumen: Real-time Global Illumination in Unreal Engine 5" and the "Lumen Technical Details" documentation) are a stochastic integration: rays are generated by importance sampling the visible GGX lobe of each pixel's material, traced through the same hybrid pipeline as the GI solve (screen traces against Scene Depth and Scene Color first, then Lumen Scene tracing with the surface cache or hardware ray tracing, skylight on miss), and denoised in screen space - spatial reuse reweights screen-space neighbors by their BRDFs, temporal accumulation reprojects and clamps the history, and a variance-driven bilateral filter runs at double strength on freshly revealed pixels with tone-mapped weights that remove fireflies. Roughness bands split the work: dedicated rays for the smooth band, world radiance-cache reuse for the rough band, and nothing above the Max Roughness To Trace. The reflection tracing runs below full resolution (a quarter ray per pixel at Lumen's High tier) with the filters reconstructing the full-resolution response, and reflections see the scene's diffuse GI because the cache they hit is lit with it.

This module reproduces that solve on the CPU, with the following documented adaptations:

- **Reflection field** - the solve runs at 1/`screen_scale` of the viewport per axis (Lumen's sub-resolution tracing scaled to CPU budgets). Each texel is the expected GGX-lobe response (importance-sampled estimator, Fresnel weight applied at trace time, the masking term left to the denoising filters - the standard real-time stochastic-SSR approximation). `copyField` writes four floats per texel: the linear-HDR radiance triplet plus the texel's surface roughness, so the renderer's bilinear sample can refuse texels from materials outside the shaded pixel's roughness band instead of blending the field across surface boundaries (texels with no response - background, unlit, above the traced roughness band - report 1.0). The renderer adds the field to shaded pixels in linear space before tone mapping; where no surrounding texel matches the pixel's material, the hemispheric ambient specular floor stands in so metallic surfaces never render pitch black.
- **Ray generation** samples the visible GGX normal distribution (Heitz 2018) in the tangent frame; a sampled microfacet below the horizon falls back to the mirror direction so the sample keeps its energy. The published roughness bands apply: `roughness.ray_band_max` (Lumen's 0.3) separates dedicated rays from the radiance-cache reuse band, and `roughness.max_trace` (Lumen's Max Roughness To Trace, 0.4) bounds where a specular environment term exists at all.
- **Screen traces** march the module's own frame GBuffer in equal world-space steps with a thin-occluder acceptance test, handing off to the scene trace on miss or when the ray leaves the screen - Lumen's "screen traces first" tier, here against the module's shaded GBuffer instead of Scene Color (the same content at field resolution). The trace length is capped by `screen_ray_distance_m`, Lumen's short-screen-ray budget.
- **Scene tracing** runs against the registered triangle scene through a top-level BVH over the surfaces plus per-surface BVHs (rays transform into each surface's local space, so dynamic transforms only rebuild the small top-level tree). Hit lighting evaluates the sun with a shadow ray, the direct lights with the renderer's attenuation formulas, the **lumen_gi world radiance cache** (validity-weighted trilinear SH2 irradiance - so reflections carry the scene's diffuse GI exactly as the renderer's own ambient does), and emission. View-dependent specular at the hit is not evaluated, matching Lumen's cached scene lighting, which carries diffuse lighting and emission.
- **Rough band** resamples the lumen_gi probe grid directionally (the probes store SH2 *radiance*; the directional polynomial evaluation is the module's own `evaluateSh2Radiance`) instead of tracing rays - the CPU counterpart of Lumen reusing its radiance caches for rough lobes, where a dedicated ray's incoherence costs more than the lobe's directional resolution needs.
- **Denoising** follows the published chain: spatial reuse averages screen-space neighbors gated by depth-plane, normal, and roughness similarity (Lumen: "reweight them based on their BRDF"); temporal accumulation reprojects the previous field through the stored camera (the history is gathered bilinearly over the reprojected footprint, each corner weighted only when it passes the depth validation, so motion no longer steps the accumulation between texels), validates it against the current depth (disocclusion), clamps the history to the current neighborhood at `temporal.neighborhood_clamp_sigma` standard deviations, and blends at `temporal.strength` - freshly revealed texels take the fresh frame whole; the bilateral filter runs only where the reuse left high variance (and at double radius on disoccluded texels), with tone-mapped weights that bound a firefly's share of the average.
- **The field composite** currently runs in the software rasterizer (the headless backend adds the sampled field to every shaded opaque pixel); the Vulkan path consumes the module through the exported field. The renderer hook itself (`renderer.reflection_indirect_specular`) is backend-agnostic - see the renderer's backends.md.

## Scene registration

Surfaces are registered through the capability with the same geometry contract as lumen_gi (borrowed vertex/index arrays, per-attribute byte strides where 0 excludes an optional attribute) plus the PBR specular parameters reflections need: `roughness` and `metallic`. The `albedoColor` is the PBR base color: the module combines it with the vertex colors exactly like the renderer's base color, uses it for the hit-point diffuse shading, and - for metallic surfaces - as the specular F0 tint the trace-time Fresnel weight scales with. Flags share lumen_gi's semantics (Unlit surfaces emit their albedo; Excluded surfaces are invisible to reflection rays - decorative sky/sun geometry the environment answers; Dynamic surfaces expect per-frame transform updates through `updateSurfaceTransform`).

## Evaluation and the renderer hook

`setView` fixes the camera (column-major view-projection, world eye, and the viewport the field will be composited onto - the field is sized from the viewport). `evaluate` runs one frame: GBuffer rasterization, the shaded screen color, the stochastic ray pass, and the denoise chain; it publishes an immutable field snapshot whose version changes only when the content does, so a renderer polls `copyField` every frame and pays the copy only on change (the lumen_gi `copyProbes` pattern). The static-frame skip returns early once the camera, scene, environment, and the lumen_gi probe grid version are all stable **and the temporal history has had the blend's convergence window to settle** - the fresh-trace share `(1 - temporal.strength)^N` falls below 1% after N clean frames, so a frozen field is always a converged one. A probe-grid version change is a dirty signal exactly like an environment change, so the field keeps re-tracing while the GI it carries converges.

## Validation views

`debugExport` writes PPM views at field resolution: Composite (the module's own shaded image plus the field, through the pbr tone mapping), ReflectionsOnly (the field isolated), ResolveMask (which tier resolved each texel: screen trace, scene trace, radiance cache, skylight), and Reference (a brute-force `debug.reference_samples`-ray-per-texel solve with the same estimator, for A/B validation of the filtered field). With more than one ray per texel, ResolveMask reports the tier of the texel's first dedicated ray.
