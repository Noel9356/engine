# lumen_reflection module

lumen_reflection implements the engine's dynamic reflections following the architecture of Unreal Engine 5's Lumen Reflections: a stochastic, GGX-importance-sampled reflection solve at reduced screen resolution, resolved through a hybrid tracing pipeline (screen traces first, then scene ray tracing, skylight on miss), with hit lighting that carries the scene's diffuse global illumination, and a three-stage screen-space denoising chain (BRDF-weighted spatial reuse, reprojection-based temporal accumulation, variance-driven bilateral filtering). The implementation is CPU-side (task-scheduler parallel); the renderer-facing result is the screen-space reflection field exposed through `copyField`, which the renderer composites onto shaded pixels as the indirect specular term.

## What Lumen Reflections actually do (and what this module adapts)

Lumen's reflections (per Epic's published design - the SIGGRAPH 2022 Advances course "Lumen: Real-time Global Illumination in Unreal Engine 5" and the "Lumen Technical Details" documentation) are a stochastic integration: rays are generated by importance sampling the visible GGX lobe of each pixel's material, traced through the same hybrid pipeline as the GI solve (screen traces against Scene Depth and Scene Color first, then Lumen Scene tracing with the surface cache or hardware ray tracing, skylight on miss), and denoised in screen space - spatial reuse reweights screen-space neighbors by their BRDFs, temporal accumulation reprojects and clamps the history, and a variance-driven bilateral filter runs at double strength on freshly revealed pixels with tone-mapped weights that remove fireflies. Roughness bands split the work: dedicated rays for the smooth band, world radiance-cache reuse for the rough band, and nothing above the Max Roughness To Trace. The reflection tracing runs below full resolution (a quarter ray per pixel at Lumen's High tier) with the filters reconstructing the full-resolution response, and reflections see the scene's diffuse GI because the cache they hit is lit with it.

This module reproduces that solve on the CPU, with the following documented adaptations:

- **Reflection field** - the solve runs at 1/`screen_scale` of the viewport per axis (Lumen's sub-resolution tracing scaled to CPU budgets). Each texel is the expected GGX-lobe response (importance-sampled estimator, Fresnel weight applied at trace time, the masking term left to the denoising filters - the standard real-time stochastic-SSR approximation). `copyField` writes four floats per texel: the linear-HDR radiance triplet plus the texel's surface roughness, so the renderer's bilinear sample can refuse texels from materials outside the shaded pixel's roughness band instead of blending the field across surface boundaries (texels with no response - background, unlit, above the traced roughness band - report the neutral 1.0). The renderer adds the field to shaded pixels in linear space before tone mapping: a pixel rougher than the field's `maxRoughness` band receives nothing (its material never traced a specular environment response), the four surrounding texels are refused when their roughness leaves the pixel's band (`reflectionFieldRoughnessBand`, the same half-width the spatial-reuse pass gates neighbors with) or carry the neutral value, and a fully-refused neighborhood reads black. Transmissive draws (material opacity below 1) use the same traced flag to composite instead of blend: the field already carries what the surface shows of the scene behind it, so blending the raw background in as well would show that scene twice.
- **Ray generation** samples the visible GGX normal distribution (Heitz 2018) in the tangent frame; a sampled microfacet below the horizon falls back to the mirror direction so the sample keeps its energy. The published roughness bands apply: `roughness.ray_band_max` (Lumen's 0.3) separates dedicated rays from the radiance-cache reuse band, and `roughness.max_trace` (Lumen's Max Roughness To Trace, 0.4) bounds where a specular environment term exists at all.
- **Screen traces** march the module's own frame GBuffer in equal world-space steps with a thin-occluder acceptance test, handing off to the scene trace on miss or when the ray leaves the screen - Lumen's "screen traces first" tier, here against the module's shaded GBuffer instead of Scene Color (the same content at field resolution). The trace length is capped by `screen_ray_distance_m`, Lumen's short-screen-ray budget.
- **Scene tracing** runs against the registered triangle scene through a top-level BVH over the surfaces plus per-surface BVHs (rays transform into each surface's local space, so dynamic transforms only rebuild the small top-level tree). Hit lighting evaluates the sun with a shadow ray, the direct lights with the renderer's attenuation formulas, the **lumen_gi world radiance cache** (validity- and tangent-plane-weighted trilinear SH2 irradiance - the shared `lumen_gi::probeTangentPlaneFactor` convention, so reflections carry the scene's diffuse GI exactly as the renderer's own ambient does), and emission. The hit's own view-dependent specular comes from the same cache: when the hit surface's roughness is inside the traced band, the probe grid answers along the mirrored view direction, Schlick-weighted by the surface's base reflectivity (the CPU counterpart of Lumen's surface cache carrying the response of reflective hits - a reflection ray landing on a mirror sees the mirror's room image, not its near-black diffuse).
- **Rough band** resamples the lumen_gi probe grid directionally (the probes store SH2 *radiance*; the directional polynomial evaluation is the module's own `evaluateSh2Radiance`) instead of tracing rays - the CPU counterpart of Lumen reusing its radiance caches for rough lobes, where a dedicated ray's incoherence costs more than the lobe's directional resolution needs.
- **Transmission** extends both tracing tiers to transparent and translucent surfaces. Screen traces pass through a transparent texel instead of stopping on it: the march accumulates the surface's attenuation (albedo times the `(1 - opacity)` transmittance, once per surface so a glass body spanning many texels attenuates once) and keeps searching for the opaque responder behind, discarding the accumulated tint when the march ends without one so the fall-back scene trace can apply the surface's refraction itself. Scene traces run ray chains: at a transparent interface the dielectric Fresnel term (from the index ratio) splits the ray stochastically into a reflected branch, resolved through a nested chain, and a transmitted branch that continues refracted by Snell's law with total internal reflection respected (a deterministic full reflect - no split runs where transmission is impossible); the interface works on the ray-facing normal (an outward normal stays outward on the exit crossing, so a sub-critical exit bends out of the medium, a super-critical one mirrors back inside on scene traces), and the reflected branch of an entry crossing may reuse the screen tier while everything inside a medium runs on scene traces; the transmitted share is tinted by the surface's albedo, applied once per medium traversal at the entering interface, and rough surfaces blur the transmitted lobe by their roughness so frosted glass reads blurry instead of clear. The branch weights divide the branch probabilities out (the pick follows the reflected branch with probability `F`, the transmitted branch with probability `(1 - F) * (1 - opacity)`, and the absorbed share `(1 - F) * opacity` otherwise), so the estimator's expectation equals the interface's composite response `F * L_reflected + (1 - F) * (1 - opacity) * albedo * L_transmitted` - the same one-split estimator the first segment uses before tracing (`rfSplitFirstSegment`, shared with the Reference debug view so the two cannot diverge). The reflected share of a transparent texel's own response splits the same way before the first trace (reflected lobe versus refracted lobe, the latter tinted), and the rough band evaluates both shares against the world radiance cache along the reflected and refracted directions. Chains cross at most `trace.max_transmission_segments` interfaces (a pane costs two: entering and exiting the medium); a chain that exhausts the budget stops tracing and the world radiance cache answers its remaining transmittance share along the would-be continuation, so deep glass chains fade toward the room instead of absorbing to black; `transmissionSegmentsThisFrame` in the statistics reports how many segments the frame walked. Shadow rays at hit points soften by the same transmittance instead of being blocked (`rfShadowTransmittance`).
- **Denoising** follows the published chain: spatial reuse averages screen-space neighbors gated by depth-plane, normal, and roughness similarity (Lumen: "reweight them based on their BRDF"); temporal accumulation reprojects the previous field through the stored camera (the history is gathered bilinearly over the reprojected footprint, each corner weighted only when it passes the depth validation, so motion no longer steps the accumulation between texels), validates it against the current depth (disocclusion), clamps the history to the current neighborhood at `temporal.neighborhood_clamp_sigma` standard deviations, and blends at `temporal.strength` - freshly revealed texels take the fresh frame whole; the bilateral filter runs only where the reuse left high variance (and at double radius on disoccluded texels), with tone-mapped weights that bound a firefly's share of the average.
- **The field composite** currently runs in the software rasterizer (the headless backend adds the sampled field to every shaded opaque pixel); the Vulkan path consumes the module through the exported field. The renderer hook itself (`renderer.reflection_indirect_specular`) is backend-agnostic - see the renderer's backends.md.

## Scene registration

Surfaces are registered through the capability with the same geometry contract as lumen_gi (borrowed vertex/index arrays, per-attribute byte strides where 0 excludes an optional attribute) plus the PBR specular parameters reflections need: `roughness` and `metallic`, and the transmission pair `opacity` (in [0, 1]; 1 is opaque, below 1 the surface transmits the remaining share tinted by the albedo) and `indexOfRefraction` (>= 1; the medium's index of refraction the refraction bends with). The `albedoColor` is the PBR base color: the module combines it with the vertex colors exactly like the renderer's base color, uses it for the hit-point diffuse shading, and - for metallic surfaces - as the specular F0 tint the trace-time Fresnel weight scales with. Flags share lumen_gi's semantics (Unlit surfaces emit their albedo; Excluded surfaces are invisible to reflection rays - decorative sky/sun geometry the environment answers; Dynamic surfaces expect per-frame transform updates through `updateSurfaceTransform`).

## Evaluation and the renderer hook

`setView` fixes the camera (column-major view-projection, world eye, and the viewport the field will be composited onto - the field is sized from the viewport). `evaluate` runs one frame: GBuffer rasterization, the shaded screen color, the stochastic ray pass, and the denoise chain; it publishes an immutable field snapshot whose version changes only when the content does, so a renderer polls `copyField` every frame and pays the copy only on change (the lumen_gi `copyProbes` pattern). The static-frame skip returns early once the camera, scene, environment, and the lumen_gi probe grid version are all stable **and the temporal history has had the blend's convergence window to settle** - the fresh-trace share `(1 - temporal.strength)^N` falls below 1% after N clean frames, so a frozen field is always a converged one. A probe-grid version change is a dirty signal exactly like an environment change, so the field keeps re-tracing while the GI it carries converges.

## Validation views

`debugExport` writes PPM views at field resolution: Composite (the module's own shaded image plus the field, through the pbr tone mapping), ReflectionsOnly (the field isolated), ResolveMask (which tier resolved each texel: screen trace, scene trace, radiance cache, skylight), and Reference (a brute-force `debug.reference_samples`-ray-per-texel solve with the same estimator, for A/B validation of the filtered field). With more than one ray per texel, ResolveMask reports the tier of the texel's first dedicated ray.
